## What is AI search?

Shoppers no longer type three words into a search bar and click through ten results. They describe their exact situation to an AI assistant: "I have sensitive skin and fragrance makes it worse, what's a good natural moisturizer?" The assistant answers with a short list of brands, usually three to five, with a reason for each and links to buy.

That short list is the new shelf. If you're on it, you get a shopper who has already compared options and been told why you fit. If you're not, a competitor gets that shopper, and you never see the lost sale in your analytics.

AI shoppers convert far better than other traffic because the research is already done. By the time they reach your store, they arrive ready to buy. That's why AI search and [conversion work](/shopify-conversion-optimization) belong together: one gets you recommended, the other makes sure the store closes the sale.

## What do I do on your site?

* **Complete structured data on every template.** Organization and WebSite on the homepage. Product and ProductGroup on product pages, with offers, ratings, reviews, identifiers (GTIN, MPN and SKU), shipping and return policy, additionalProperty for product attributes, and certifications where relevant. BreadcrumbList across the site, ItemList on collections, FAQPage on FAQ content, and Article on blog posts.
* **Fixing what your existing sections emit.** Most themes output partial or outdated schema. I fix it at the source so it stays right as the catalog changes.
* **Removing duplicate and conflicting app schema.** Review, bundle and subscription apps often add their own markup. I combine it into one complete, consistent record per product.
* **Product attributes based on what AI asks.** I run buyer questions for each product type and add the attributes AI needs to recommend you for them, built into your theme so it stays accurate as your catalog changes.
* **Answer first product content and FAQs**, written from your customers' own language.
* **AI crawler access.** robots.txt, bot protection settings, your sitemap and an llms.txt file, so AI crawlers can read everything you want them to.

## What do I do off site?

* **Finding the sources AI cites in your category.** The buyer question test shows exactly which lists, reviews, articles and threads AI leans on when it recommends your competitors.
* **Getting you into them.** Affiliate and publisher outreach to the sites AI already trusts in your category.
* **Review strategy**, so the platforms AI reads reflect what customers love about your products.
* **Amazon listing improvements**, so your retail listings tell AI the same story your store does.

## How is AI search measured?

* **50 buyer questions** built from your customers' language: reviews, support tickets and post purchase survey answers. I run them repeatedly and track your mention rate, your position in each answer, whether the answer is accurate, and which sources are cited.
* **AI referral traffic** from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Copilot in your analytics.
* **A post purchase survey** question, because most AI driven visits show up as direct traffic.
* **Branded search and direct traffic growth**, which rise as more shoppers hear your name from AI.
* **The high converting visitor segment** that AI research produces, so you can see what those shoppers are worth.

## Why isn't schema enough?

Schema is the part of AI search a developer can add in an afternoon, and you should have it. But it's one of four things AI weighs, and on its own it rarely changes which brands get named. AI recommends the brands it can read, trust and verify:

* **Product data and schema**, complete enough that AI knows what each product is and who it's for.
* **Answer first content** that matches the questions shoppers actually ask.
* **Crawler access**, so AI can reach the pages at all.
* **Off site sources**: the reviews, lists, press and forums that back you up.

Then the store has to turn that shopper into an order. Knowing which buyer questions matter in your category, which sources AI trusts there, and how to convert the traffic is the work. I do all of it, measure it against real buyer questions, and focus where you can win.
