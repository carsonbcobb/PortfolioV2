## What do I fix on product pages?

The product page is where most sales are won or lost, so it's where I spend the most time.

I look at what a shopper sees in the first screen on mobile: the product, the price, the main reason to buy, and a clear button. Then I work down the page. Benefits written the way customers talk. Ingredients, sizing or specs where people expect them. Reviews placed where doubt shows up. Shipping and return details close to the button, not buried in the footer.

For products people reorder, I make sure subscribe and save is visible and set up well. My [Shopify subscriptions page](/shopify-subscriptions) covers that in detail.

## What about cart and checkout?

The cart is your last chance to raise order value before the customer pays. Most carts waste it.

I set up the cart so it does three jobs: shows progress toward free shipping, offers one relevant add on, and gets out of the way. With Rebuy or the theme, I pick upsells based on what people actually buy together, not a random best seller.

At checkout, I focus on what you control: making sure discounts apply cleanly, shipping options are clear, trust signals are in place, and nothing surprises the shopper at the last step.

## How much does mobile speed matter?

Speed is where I find some of the biggest wins, because a slow page loses people before they see anything.

Most slow Shopify stores aren't slowed down by the theme. They're slowed down by app scripts, oversized images and leftover code from apps uninstalled long ago. I audit every script, cut what isn't earning its place, compress and resize images, and defer anything that doesn't need to load first.

One supplement brand I worked with had product pages taking 7.9 seconds to load on mobile. After the cleanup they loaded in 2.1 seconds, and conversion rose 24%. Same ads, same products, same prices.

## How do testing and measurement work?

Before I change anything, I check the tracking. If orders, add to carts and revenue aren't recorded correctly, every decision after that is a guess.

Once the numbers are right and the obvious problems are fixed, I build a testing roadmap. Each test has a clear idea behind it, a single change to measure, and enough traffic to read the result. Then I ship the winners and write down what we learned so the next test is smarter.

If your store doesn't have the traffic for clean tests yet, that's fine. I'll fix the clear problems first and introduce testing when it makes sense.
