## What a fractional head of ecommerce actually does

The title sounds bigger than it is. In practice it means one person leads the work on how your Shopify store makes money, and that person is me. You still make the calls.

That covers:

* **The roadmap.** What we fix, build and test each month, ranked by revenue impact, not by who asked loudest. I propose, you decide.
* **The numbers.** Conversion rate, average order value, subscription share, repeat rate, and the tracking underneath them so we can trust what we see.
* **The store.** Product pages, collections, cart, speed, merchandising and promos. I do most of this work myself, and nothing goes live without your sign off.
* **AI search and conversion.** How ChatGPT and Google's AI answers describe and recommend you, and how well the store turns that traffic into orders.
* **The stack.** Shopify, Recharge or Skio, Rebuy and Klaviyo, plus every other app you're paying for. I keep what earns its place and remove what doesn't.
* **The vendors.** Designers, developers and agencies get clear briefs from me, and I check their work before it ships. One point of contact for you.
* **The reporting.** A short, plain weekly update on what moved, what didn't and what's next.

You stop being the project manager for your own website.

## Fractional vs full time vs agency: when you need which

There's no single right answer. Here's how I'd think about it.

**A full time head of ecommerce** makes sense when the channel needs someone inside the business every day, leading a team of their own. A typical full time salary for the role is $150K+ a year before benefits, and it can take months to find the right person. For a brand that's growing but not yet at that scale, it's a lot of fixed cost for one role.

**An agency** makes sense when you have a clearly scoped project, like a full redesign, and someone on your team who can manage it. Agencies are built to deliver projects. They're less often built to lead your numbers month after month.

**A fractional head of ecommerce** makes sense when you need a senior lead for the store without a senior salary. You get someone who sets direction and does the work, on a retainer you can adjust as you grow.

Many brands end up with a mix. I've written a longer, honest version of this in my [agency vs fractional head of ecommerce comparison](/agency-vs-fractional-head-of-ecommerce), including when I think an agency is the better pick.

## What the first 90 days look like

I work the same way on almost every store, because the order matters.

**Month 1: tracking, speed and key pages.** I make sure the numbers are right before I change anything. Then I fix the pages that carry the most revenue, usually the hero product pages and the cart, and clean up whatever is slowing them down on mobile. More on that on my [conversion optimization page](/shopify-conversion-optimization).

**Month 2: order value.** Upsells and bundles in Rebuy or your theme, a real subscription offer in Recharge or Skio, and Klaviyo flows that bring people back. If subscriptions are part of your business, my [Shopify subscriptions page](/shopify-subscriptions) covers how I approach them.

**Month 3: testing roadmap and promo calendar.** By now I know your store well enough to plan tests that matter and to map out promos ahead of time, so the next sale isn't put together the night before.

After 90 days the work becomes a steady rhythm: test, fix, ship, report.
