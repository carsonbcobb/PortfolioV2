A shopper in Ohio asks ChatGPT: "Which Korean moisturizer is gentle enough for really sensitive skin, and can I get it shipped fast in the US?" The answer names a few products and a reason for each. If your brand is the one Korean shoppers already love but American shoppers can't find, that answer goes to someone else.

Korean skincare is one clear example. The same applies to any brand selling into the US from abroad, whether you make supplements in Australia, apparel in the UK or snacks in Japan.

## What's different when US shoppers ask AI?

American shoppers ask in English and with US context. They want to know whether it ships to the US and how fast, what it costs in dollars, whether it's available in the US right now, and whether the ingredients and labeling meet what they expect. AI answers from what it can find, so a product page written for your home market, or translated without that context, leaves AI without the facts to recommend you.

The questions are also different. A shopper who has never heard of your brand doesn't search your product name. They describe their problem, their skin or their budget, and the AI picks from the brands it can match to that description.

## What AI recommends to US shoppers today

I asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode 20 real US shopper questions about Korean skincare. Three findings stand out for any brand selling into the US:

- **A few brands win almost every answer.** COSRX, Beauty of Joseon, Anua and Round Lab appeared in 56 of 60 answers (93%).
- **The brand's own store rarely gets the click.** Of 241 product cards, the brand's own store was the seller on 37 (15%). Resellers and US retailers had most of the rest.
- **Each AI trusts different sources.** Across 25 Google AI Mode answers, Reddit threads were 20 of the 39 sources shown on screen. ChatGPT cited Ulta in 13 of 20 answers, more than any other site.

The full results are in [which Korean skincare brands AI recommends to US shoppers](/reports/korean-skincare-ai-recommendations-us-2026).

## Which sources does AI trust for US shoppers?

Mostly US ones. ChatGPT showed a source in [45 of 48 product answers](/reports/sources-chatgpt-cites-2026) I checked, led by review sites, testing sites and magazines. For a brand that's famous at home, that's the gap: coverage in your home market's press and forums rarely shows up when an American shopper asks. In beauty, community sources like Reddit and YouTube got [the largest share of Google AI Mode's links](/reports/where-ai-sends-shoppers-2026), so US community threads matter too.

## What does your store need for AI to recommend you in the US?

- **A price in dollars that AI can read.** In the page HTML and in the product's structured data, not only after a currency script runs. More on [how AI shows prices](/reports/prices-in-ai-shopping-answers-2026).
- **US shipping and availability, stated plainly.** Whether you ship to the US, how long it takes, and what it costs, on the product page and in your shipping policy.
- **Product pages that answer US questions.** Who it's for, how to use it, and ingredients in the terms US shoppers use, within what your brand can legally claim in the US.
- **A clean setup for more than one market.** If you sell in several countries from one Shopify store, AI and Google need to tell your US pages apart. See [how Shopify brands handle hreflang](/reports/shopify-international-hreflang-2026).
- **A page that converts American shoppers.** AI sends them straight to a product page, already told what to expect. If it doesn't confirm US shipping and price fast, they leave.

## Example: a Korean skincare brand selling into the US

Say a Korean brand sells a gentle moisturizer that does well at home. In the US, I'd start by asking ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode the questions American shoppers ask about sensitive skin, more than once each, and recording who gets named and which sources each answer cites. Then I'd compare the competitor's US product page with yours on the facts in the question: skin type, what's left out, price in dollars, US shipping.

The fixes usually come in that order. Make the page answer the American shopper's question, make the US price and shipping readable, then work on getting the product into the US review sites, magazines and threads that AI already trusts for that question.
