Food and drink brands have a gift most categories would love. People finish the product and want more. A bag of coffee lasts a couple of weeks. A jar of hot sauce gets emptied. A case of drinks disappears.

The question is whether your store catches that reorder, or lets the customer drift back to the grocery store.

## The problems I see in food and beverage stores

### Subscriptions that don't match how people eat and drink

Coffee drinkers go through beans at very different speeds. A snack box might last one household a week and another a month. If your subscription only offers every 30 days, a lot of people will end up with too much, skip a few times, and cancel.

I set frequencies from your real reorder gaps, make it easy to skip, change cadence or swap flavors, and set up a cancel flow that offers those options before goodbye. I work in Recharge, Skio and native Shopify selling plans. My [Shopify subscriptions page](/shopify-subscriptions) goes deeper, and if you're picking an app, start with my [Recharge vs Skio vs Loop comparison](/recharge-vs-skio-vs-loop).

### Bundles that are hard to build

Variety packs, sampler boxes and build your own bundles are some of the best order value tools in food and drink. They're also easy to set up badly. Inventory doesn't track by component, discounts stack in strange ways, or the bundle can't be subscribed to.

I set up bundles that track inventory correctly, show the savings clearly, and work with subscriptions. With Rebuy or the theme, I show the right bundle or add on on the product page and in the cart.

### Free shipping lines set in the wrong spot

Food and drink products are often low priced and heavy, so shipping is a big deal for margins. Many stores pick a free shipping threshold at launch and never revisit it.

If a typical cart lands just under the line, people feel nickel and dimed. If the line is far above a typical cart, nobody tries to reach it. I look at your real cart values, product prices and shipping costs, and set the line where one more item gets people over it. Then I show progress toward it in the cart with a suggested add on that closes the gap.

### Gifting season built at the last minute

For a lot of food and beverage brands, the holidays are the biggest stretch of the year. Gift sets, gift cards, corporate orders and ship by dates all matter at once.

What I usually find is that gift sets get built the week before, the gift collection is hard to find, and ship by dates live in an email but not on the site. I plan gifting on a promo calendar weeks ahead: products, a clear gift collection, gift messages at checkout where your setup allows, ship by dates on the product page and cart, and Klaviyo emails timed to each one.

### Perishable shipping that surprises customers

If you sell anything that can melt, spoil or go stale, the store has to set expectations. When it doesn't, you get support tickets, refunds and bad reviews for problems that started at checkout.

On the store side I set up shipping rules and order cutoffs that match your fulfillment schedule, so orders don't sit over a weekend. I add clear delivery messages on the product page and in the cart, and send emails that tell customers what to expect and when. Your fulfillment partner owns the packing. I make sure the store and the warehouse are telling the same story.

### Product pages that don't sell the taste

You can't taste a product through a screen. The page has to do that work: flavor notes in plain words, how people use it, what it pairs with, and reviews that talk about taste. Most pages lean on a pretty photo and a paragraph of brand story.

I rebuild product pages for mobile with the reasons to buy up top, and use Shopify metafields for things like roast level, heat level, ingredients and allergens so they stay consistent across the catalog.
