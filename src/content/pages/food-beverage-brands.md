Food and drink shoppers now ask AI the way they'd ask a friend: "I'm gluten free and want a granola that's actually crunchy and not loaded with sugar." Or: "I need healthy snack bars for my kids' lunchboxes that they'll actually eat, without a ton of sugar, and nut free for school."

The AI answers with a few products and a reason for each. In food and beverage, it often links straight to the brand's own product page. So the page that shopper lands on has to confirm everything they asked for, in their words, before they lose interest.

## What I found when I tested products like yours

In November 2026 I asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode the questions food and drink shoppers ask, and checked the product pages and product data behind the answers.

- **AI sends food shoppers straight to brand websites.** On Google AI Mode, [63% of links in food and beverage answers went to brand websites](/reports/where-ai-sends-shoppers-2026), more than any other product type, often straight to the product or collection page.
- **The three AIs agree least on food.** [Only 16% of the food and beverage brands named came from all three](/reports/chatgpt-gemini-ai-mode-brand-overlap-2026), the lowest of any product type. Showing up on one AI tells you little about the others.
- **Most products can't be matched by barcode.** [63% of food and beverage products I checked had no barcode on any variant](/reports/shopify-catalog-data-ai-shopping-2026), the most of any product type.
- **Pages don't say how to eat it or who it's for.** [None of the 16 food and beverage product pages I checked](/reports/do-product-pages-answer-ai-shopper-questions) explained how to use or serve the product, and 25% said who it's for.
- **Wording changes the answer.** When I rewrote short questions the way shoppers really ask, ChatGPT kept the same top pick in [only 9 of 24 questions](/reports/how-question-wording-changes-ai-recommendations). The details a shopper adds change who gets recommended.

## What AI looks for on your product pages

When a parent asks for nut free snack bars their kids will eat, the AI looks for a page that says nut free, says it's made for kids, and says something about taste. These are the gaps I find most often on food and beverage pages.

- **Who it's for.** Gluten free, vegan, kids, athletes, people cutting sugar. Shoppers lead with this, and most pages leave it to the photo.
- **Ingredients and allergens, written out.** Allergen facts that live only on the package image give AI less to read than the same facts written on the page.
- **How to eat, serve or prepare it.** Brew ratios, serving ideas, what it pairs with. Shoppers ask, and AI can't answer from a page that doesn't say.
- **Taste in plain words.** Crunchy, not too sweet, mild or hot. A page that only says "delicious" can't be matched to a shopper who asked for crunchy.
- **An honest comparison.** How your granola differs from the one next to it on the shelf. Few pages say.
- **Reviews that talk about taste.** Reviews that mention crunch, sweetness or kids who liked it help AI connect your product to the shopper who asked about exactly that.

Every gap is a question your page leaves AI to answer from someone else's site, and in food and drink, AI is most likely to send the shopper to your own page to check.
