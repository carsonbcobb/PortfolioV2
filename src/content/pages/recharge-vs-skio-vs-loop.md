If you sell something people reorder on Shopify, you'll end up choosing between a short list of subscription apps. Recharge, Skio and Loop are the three I get asked about most. Recharge announced on April 27, 2026 that Skio is joining Recharge.

I'm Carson Cobb. I help Shopify brands get recommended in ChatGPT and Google's AI answers, then make the store convert that traffic, and I set up and fix subscription programs as part of my work. I've spent the most time inside Recharge and Skio. I don't get paid by any of these vendors, and I'll tell you where each one fits and where it doesn't.

One thing first. The app is rarely the reason a subscription program underperforms. Most of the leaks I find in audits, like a hero product with no subscribe option or a widget that never renders on the product page, would follow you to any app. If that sounds like your store, start with my [Shopify subscriptions page](/shopify-subscriptions), or put your numbers into the [subscription revenue calculator](/tools/subscription-calculator).

## How does Skio compare to Recharge?

Skio has one public plan with most features included, at $499/month billed annually or $599/month billed monthly, plus 1.25% + 20¢ per subscription order. Recharge starts lower at $149/month, but bundles, SMS and migration support only come with its $499/month Scale plan. Both are now owned by Recharge.

On fees, Skio and Recharge Scale charge the same 1.25% + 20¢. Recharge Starter costs less each month but charges 1.50% + 20¢ per transaction. Skio's Scale plan includes its multi step cancel flow builder, advanced build a box, SkioSMS for one and two way texting, and an AI driven upsell carousel. Recharge puts smart cancellation prevention and its customer portal on every plan, and saves bundles, Concierge SMS, gifting and its JavaScript SDK for Scale and Custom. Skio charges extra for its loyalty program and doesn't publish the price.

The full breakdown is on my [Recharge vs Skio page](/recharge-vs-skio).

## How does Loop compare to Recharge?

Loop costs less to start and charges lower percentage fees. Its Starter plan is $99/month plus 1.0%, and Pro is $399/month plus 0.75%, against Recharge at $149/month plus 1.50% + 20¢ or $499/month plus 1.25% + 20¢. Loop includes white glove migration on every paid plan. Recharge includes migration support on Scale.

Loop has one fee Recharge doesn't: 15¢ per loyalty order on Starter and 10¢ on Pro. It bills month to month. Recharge bills monthly too, and its pricing FAQ says its Custom plan runs on a 12 month term. Loop includes personalized cancellation flows and build your own bundles on every plan. Recharge needs Scale for bundles. Loop doesn't have its own SMS product, and on Pro it connects with SMS tools like Postscript and Attentive. Recharge has Concierge SMS on Scale, billed at $0.03 per message segment in the US and Canada and $0.06 internationally.

The full breakdown is on my [Recharge vs Loop page](/recharge-vs-loop).

## How does Skio compare to Loop?

Loop is cheaper at every public tier. Starter is $99/month plus 1.0%, and Pro is $399/month plus 0.75%, against Skio at $499/month billed annually plus 1.25% + 20¢ per subscription order. Skio includes SMS and a full feature set on one plan. Loop includes white glove migration on every paid plan.

Skio's appeal is that one plan covers almost everything: the cancel flow builder, build a box, SMS, upsells and analytics. Loop splits features between Starter and Pro. Cancellation flow A/B testing, win back campaigns, personalized upsells and its bundle SDK are on Pro. Both include migration help, and both charge extra for loyalty: Loop per loyalty order, Skio as an add on. The other difference now is ownership. Skio is part of Recharge, and Loop is independent.

The full breakdown is on my [Skio vs Loop page](/skio-vs-loop).

## What changes now that Recharge owns Skio?

Not much yet. Recharge announced on April 27, 2026 that Skio is joining Recharge. Recharge says nothing changes today for merchants on either platform, both will keep operating as they have, and your existing team stays your point of contact. Skio's listing is now titled Skio, a Recharge Company.

Recharge's announcement says it will review elements of both platforms over the coming months, with more details to come. Skio's own announcement says both platforms will keep shipping features, and that over roughly the next 12 months it will work with Recharge to combine the best of both. Neither company has said either platform will be shut down.

What that means in practice is still unclear. If you're choosing Skio specifically because it's an alternative to Recharge, factor that in. If you're happy on either one, there's no reason to panic. I cover this in more detail in [what the Recharge and Skio deal means for brands](/recharge-skio-acquisition).

## Which one I'd pick at your order volume

At low volume, I'd pick Loop for its free plan and $99 Starter. As subscriptions grow and you want the most established platform, Recharge Scale. If the portal and cancel flow are the business, Skio. At high volume, the percentage fee matters most, so compare Loop Pro at 0.75% against custom quotes from all three.

**A newer brand just launching subscriptions.** Loop. The Free Forever plan up to 50 active subscriptions and the $99/month Starter plan with a 1.0% fee are the lowest public entry point of the three, and bundles and cancel flows are included from Starter.

**A growing brand that wants the most established platform and a deep ecosystem.** Recharge. It has by far the most reviews on the Shopify App Store and the most built in add ons on Scale. Just know that migration support, bundles and SMS sit on Scale, which is $499/month.

**A brand where subscribers are the core of the business and the portal experience matters most.** Skio. One plan with most features included, SMS built in, and a strong cancel flow builder. The trade off is a higher starting price and uncertainty about how it fits with Recharge long term.

**A brand doing high subscription volume and watching fees closely.** Run the math on Loop Pro at 0.75% against Skio and Recharge Scale at 1.25% + 20¢, then get custom quotes from all three. At high volume the percentage and per order fees matter far more than the monthly fee.

**A brand already on one of these and unhappy.** Don't switch yet. Fix the offer, the product page widget, the default selection and the cancel flow first. If the app still can't do what you need after that, then migrate.

## What switching costs, and how to migrate without losing subscribers

The vendors' migration help is mostly free. Loop charges no setup, migration or onboarding fees on paid plans, Skio says free migration is included, and Recharge includes migration support on Scale. The real cost is risk: missed charges, broken payment methods and confused subscribers if the cutover goes wrong.

Here's how I'd plan it:

* **Time the cutover around your billing calendar.** Move between big billing days, not on one.
* **Check the payment methods move cleanly.** A subscriber whose card doesn't come across won't be charged, and you won't notice until churn goes up.
* **Test real subscriptions before go live.** Place, skip, swap and cancel a few on the new app before you move everyone.
* **Rebuild the flows, not just the data.** Cancel flows, upsells, bundles and email triggers don't move on their own.
* **Tell subscribers what changes.** A short email about the new portal saves a lot of support tickets.
* **Watch the first full billing cycle.** Compare charges, failures and cancellations with the cycle before.

Loop says it has helped more than 1,100 brands migrate, and Recharge says it has migrated more than 53 million subscriptions. Whichever you pick, someone on your side should own the cutover. That's work I do for clients as part of a retainer.
