Pet owners are some of the most loyal customers there are. Once a dog does well on a food, or a cat finally likes a treat, the owner doesn't want to switch. They want the same thing, on time, every time.

That loyalty is a gift. But only if the store makes reordering easy. Too many pet stores make owners remember, come back, find the product and check out again, every few weeks, forever.

## The problems I see in pet stores

### Autoship that doesn't match the bag

A 4 lb bag lasts a small dog a long time. A 24 lb bag lasts a big dog a few weeks. Two dogs halve that. Yet most pet stores offer the same autoship options on every size: every 30 days, take it or leave it.

Owners end up with too much food, skip a few times, then cancel. I set autoship frequencies from bag size, serving size and your real reorder gaps, and give owners easy ways to skip, pause, change frequency or swap the size before they ever think about cancelling. I work in Recharge, Skio and native Shopify selling plans. My [Shopify subscriptions page](/shopify-subscriptions) covers how I set these up, and my [Recharge vs Skio vs Loop comparison](/recharge-vs-skio-vs-loop) helps if you're choosing an app.

### Trial packs that lead nowhere

Switching a pet's food is a big decision. A trial pack or sample bag lowers the risk, and a lot of brands sell one. Then the trial arrives, the pet loves it, and there's no clear next step.

I connect trial packs to the full size product and to autoship. That can be a post purchase offer, a Klaviyo sequence timed to when the trial runs out, or a starter offer that rolls into autoship. No happy trial customer should have to go looking for how to reorder.

### Bundles that don't fit how owners shop

Pet owners buy in combinations. Food and treats. A supplement and a dental chew. A puppy starter kit. But most stores sell each item alone and hope the owner adds the rest.

I set up bundles that make sense for your catalog, track inventory per component, work with autoship, and show the savings clearly. With Rebuy or the theme, I show the right add on on the product page and in the cart, based on what owners actually buy together.

### Sold out products that kill the sale

When your best food or treat sells out, the damage is bigger than one lost order. A pet owner who can't get their usual food goes and buys something else, and their pet might stay on it.

I make sure sold out never means dead end:

* A back in stock signup on the product page that feeds into Klaviyo.
* A follow up email the moment stock returns.
* A related product or preorder where it makes sense.
* A plan for autoship orders that fall during a stockout, so owners hear from you before their order is affected.

### Product pages that don't answer owner questions

Owners want to know what's in it, how much to feed, whether it's right for their pet's age or size, and whether other owners had good results. Most pages answer some of that in a long paragraph.

I rebuild product pages for mobile with the key answers up top, feeding guides and ingredients in clear sections, and reviews where doubt shows up. Details like life stage, size, protein source and feeding amounts are built into your site, so they stay accurate as your catalog changes. For pet supplements, I build clear places for the claims your team has approved. I don't write or approve claims myself.

### Slow pages on mobile

Most pet traffic comes from phones. Pet stores tend to collect apps for reviews, quizzes, subscriptions, loyalty and popups, and each one adds weight. I audit every script, cut what isn't earning its place, and compress images until the product page loads fast.
