Pet owners ask AI about their own animal: "My dog has a sensitive stomach and I'm thinking about switching to fresh dog food, but it needs to be worth the price." Or: "My rescue dog panics during thunderstorms. I want a calming chew that actually helps before trying medication."

The AI answers with a few products and a reason for each. If your product isn't one of them, that owner never sees your site. If it is, they arrive with a specific dog in mind, and your product page has to confirm the product is right for that dog.

## What I found when I tested products like yours

In November 2026 I asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode the questions pet owners ask, and checked the product pages and product data behind the answers.

- **Retailers matter more in pet than anywhere else.** Pet was the one product type where [retailers took a real share of Google AI Mode's links, 20%](/reports/where-ai-sends-shoppers-2026), alongside 34% to brand websites.
- **The three AIs rarely agree on pet brands.** [Only 17% of the pet brands named came from all three](/reports/chatgpt-gemini-ai-mode-brand-overlap-2026). Showing up on one AI tells you little about the others.
- **Pages skip fit and use.** [Only 13% of pet product pages I checked explained how to use the product](/reports/do-product-pages-answer-ai-shopper-questions), and 20% had a size or fit guide, even though fit and use are what dog owners ask about.
- **Some products are hard to match.** [20% of pet products I checked had no barcode on any variant](/reports/shopify-catalog-data-ai-shopping-2026), which makes it harder for Google to connect your product to the same product on retailer and review sites.
- **AI gets ratings wrong.** ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode got a product's rating and review count right on all three for [only 7 of 22 products](/reports/what-ai-gets-wrong-about-products-2026).

## What AI looks for on your product pages

When an owner asks for a calming chew for a rescue dog that panics in storms, the AI looks for a page that says who the chew is for and when to give it. These are the gaps I find most often on pet pages.

- **Who it's for.** Age, size, breed, sensitive stomach, anxious dogs. Owners lead with their pet, and most pages lead with the brand story.
- **Ingredients and protein source, written out.** Owners switching food ask what's in it. A label shown only as an image gives AI less to read than the same facts on the page.
- **How to feed or use it.** Feeding amounts by weight, how to switch foods, when to give a calming chew. Owners ask, and AI can't answer from a page that doesn't say.
- **Size and fit.** Harnesses, beds, crates and bowls all depend on the dog's size. Without a fit guide, AI has nothing to match.
- **An honest comparison.** Fresh food against kibble, or a chew against medication. Owners ask whether it's worth the price, and few pages help AI answer.
- **Reviews from owners with the same problem.** Reviews that mention a sensitive stomach or storm anxiety help AI connect your product to the owner who asked about exactly that.

Every gap is a question your page leaves AI to answer from someone else's site, and in pet, that site may be a retailer selling the product next to yours.
