Apparel shoppers describe the fit before they ever see a brand: "Breathable linen pants for summer travel that don't wrinkle in a suitcase." Or: "Jeans that fit petite women with a curvy build, without gaping at the waist."

The AI answers with a few products and a reason for each. If your product isn't one of them, that shopper never sees your site. If it is, they arrive expecting the exact fit and fabric AI described, and your product page has to confirm it fast.

I'm Carson Cobb, an independent [AI search](/ai-search) and CRO specialist for Shopify brands, working with brands in every product area. I look at how AI answers the questions your buyers ask, category by category, then make the store turn those visitors into orders. You can see where you stand today with the [AI Visibility Check](/ai-visibility-check).

## What I found when I tested products like yours

In November 2026 I asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode the questions apparel shoppers ask, and scanned the product pages and product data behind the answers.

- **Few apparel stores get named.** [Only 6 of 96 apparel stores on Shopify were named](/reports/ai-search-apparel-brands-2026) at least once, 6%, against 11% across all six product areas.
- **The technical setup is strong.** 93% of apparel stores carry product structured data and 56% have variant data with ProductGroup, the best of any area. The gap is in what the pages say.
- **Brand sites get the click.** In apparel answers, [56% of Google AI Mode's links went to brand websites](/reports/where-ai-sends-shoppers-2026), so your own pages matter more here than in most areas.
- **The three AIs agree on few brands.** [23% of the apparel brands named came from all three](/reports/chatgpt-gemini-ai-mode-brand-overlap-2026).
- **Duplicate product data is most common in apparel.** [44% of apparel stores](/reports/duplicate-product-schema-ai-visibility) published two or three Product blocks on their best selling product page.

## AEO for apparel brands: what it takes

AEO, or answer engine optimization, is getting your product into the answer when a shopper asks AI what to buy. Apparel questions are attribute driven: fit, fabric, sizing, occasion, climate, body type. AI can only match your product to that question when the answer is readable on the product page and in the structured data.

Most apparel stores keep those details where AI can't use them. Fit lives in a model photo. Fabric weight lives in a size chart image. Sizing lives in a line like "true to size" that says nothing about who it fits. So the AI names the brand whose page spells it out, or the reviewer who wore it.

What I do for apparel brands:

- **Structured attributes for fit, fabric, care and sizing,** built from metafields so your team can edit them in Shopify, and carried into the [structured data](/shopify-structured-data) for every variant.
- **Product pages that answer fit and sizing questions directly:** who it fits, how it runs, what it's made of, how it holds up, what to wear it for.
- **Consistent data across the variants of each style,** so every size and color has its own price, availability and identifier, and AI never mixes them up.
- **The sources AI trusts describing your brand correctly:** fit reviewers, testers and the threads where real wearers compare.
- **Then making sure the shoppers AI sends actually convert.**

## What AI looks for on your product pages

When a shopper asks for jeans that fit a petite, curvy build, the AI looks for a page that says petite, describes the rise and the waist, and has reviews from people with the same build. These are the gaps I find most often on apparel pages.

- **Fit, in words.** Rise, inseam, cut and how it runs, stated on the page, not only shown on a model.
- **Fabric and weight.** What it's made of, how it feels, whether it breathes, stretches or wrinkles.
- **Who it's for.** Body type, height and the occasion or climate it suits. Shoppers lead with their situation.
- **Sizing help AI can read.** A size chart as text, not an image, and a plain line on how it runs.
- **Care and durability.** How it washes and how it holds up, the question behind most apparel comparisons.
- **Reviews that mention fit.** Reviews that say "runs small" or "great for tall frames" help AI connect your product to the shopper who asked about exactly that.

Every gap is a question your page leaves AI to answer from someone else's site.

## Then the sale

AI referred shoppers arrive ready to buy. [Shopify reported in May 2026](/reports/ai-search-statistics-2026) that AI referred visitors who land on a product page convert about 49% more often than organic search visitors. The page has to confirm what AI told them: the fit, the fabric, the size that works for them. That's where my [conversion optimization](/shopify-conversion-optimization) work starts: size pickers that show what's in stock, fit notes next to the size buttons, and a fast mobile page. For a women's intimates brand, the [mobile performance score went from 31 to 85](/results).

Sold out sizes lose apparel shoppers quietly. In my store checks, [9 of 14 sold out apparel pages offered a back in stock signup](/reports/shopify-store-benchmarks-2026#back-in-stock-alerts), so the rest let a shopper who wanted that size walk away. The [Full Store Audit](/full-store-audit) reviews sold out pages, size pickers and every other page of the store, with every fix ranked by what it's worth.
