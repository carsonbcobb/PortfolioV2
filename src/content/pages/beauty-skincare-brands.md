Skincare shoppers describe their skin to AI before they ever see a brand: "My skin gets red and stings with most moisturizers. Which Korean moisturizer is gentle enough for really sensitive skin?" Or: "I'm 28 and still getting breakouts. I want an exfoliating cleanser that helps without drying my skin out."

The AI answers with a few products and a reason for each. If your product isn't one of them, that shopper never sees your site. If it is, they arrive expecting a product made for their skin, and your product page has to confirm it fast.

## What I found when I tested products like yours

In November 2026 I asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode the questions beauty and skincare shoppers ask, and checked the product pages and product data behind the answers.

- **Community sources outrank brand sites.** In beauty and skincare, community sources like Reddit and YouTube got [the largest share of Google AI Mode's links, 30%](/reports/where-ai-sends-shoppers-2026), ahead of brand websites at 28%.
- **The three AIs agree on only some brands.** [25% of the beauty and skincare brands named came from all three](/reports/chatgpt-gemini-ai-mode-brand-overlap-2026). For a question about ingrown hair, none of the three named a brand.
- **Pages explain use but skip comparisons.** [63% of beauty product pages I checked explain how to use the product](/reports/do-product-pages-answer-ai-shopper-questions), the best of any product type, but none linked a size guide and 19% compared the product to anything.
- **Product data is strong.** [81% of beauty products I checked had barcodes on every variant](/reports/shopify-catalog-data-ai-shopping-2026). The gaps are on the page, not in the data.
- **AI gets ratings wrong.** ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode got a product's rating and review count right on all three for [only 7 of 22 products](/reports/what-ai-gets-wrong-about-products-2026).

## What AI looks for on your product pages

When a shopper asks for a moisturizer that won't sting sensitive skin, the AI looks for a page that says sensitive skin, says what's left out, and has reviews from people with the same problem. These are the gaps I find most often on beauty pages.

- **Who it's for.** Skin type, concern and age. Shoppers lead with their skin, and many pages lead with the brand story.
- **Ingredients, with the reason they're there.** A full ingredient list helps, but shoppers ask what a product leaves out and what each key ingredient does.
- **Where it fits in a routine.** Most pages explain how to use the product. Fewer say what to pair it with, or when not to.
- **Shade and size.** Shades and sizes that are only shown in photos, not described in words, are hard for AI to tell apart.
- **An honest comparison.** Shoppers ask AI how one product compares to another, and few pages help it answer.
- **Reviews that match the question.** Reviews that mention sensitive skin or breakouts help AI connect your product to the shopper who asked about exactly that, and community sources fill the gap when your page doesn't.

Every gap is a question your page leaves AI to answer from someone else's site. In beauty, that's usually a forum thread or a video you didn't make.
