**By Carson Cobb. Data collected November 14, 2026.**

This month Google told investors it is building "one seamless experience across AI Overviews, AI Mode, and Traditional Search." A lot of founders read that as good news: if you already rank on Google, the AI answers will take care of themselves.

So I tested it. I ran 19 real shopping questions, like the ones customers type right before they buy, through Google search and Google AI Mode on the same day, from the same US session, and compared who showed up in each.

## The short version

- **Only 40% of the sites AI Mode cited ranked on Google's first page** for the same question (36 of 91).
- **Google's number one result was cited in AI Mode for just 7 of 19 questions.**
- **11 of 19 AI Mode answers were built from product listings,** each with a price, star rating, review count and seller, not from articles.
- **Brand stores win more often in AI Mode.** They were 16% of Google's first page results but 37% of the sites AI Mode cited.
- **Small brands get in.** 27 of 60 product listings were sold by the brand's own store, and several had fewer than 60 reviews.
- **Reddit dominates Google, not AI Mode.** It was the top Google result for 11 of 19 questions but was cited in AI Mode only 4 times.

## Google's first page vs AI Mode

| What I compared | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Sites AI Mode cited that also ranked on Google's first page | 36 of 91 (40%) |
| Questions where Google's top result was cited in AI Mode | 7 of 19 |
| Questions where AI Mode cited no site from Google's first page | 4 of 19 |
| AI Mode answers built from product listings | 11 of 19 |

Ranking helps, since 4 in 10 cited sites did rank. But 6 in 10 sites AI Mode leaned on weren't on Google's first page at all for that question. AI Mode breaks a question into many smaller searches and builds one answer from what those turn up, so it pulls in pages that never rank for the main phrase.

## Who each one shows

| Type of site | Share of Google's first page | Share of AI Mode citations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Publishers and expert sites | 47% | 38% |
| Brand stores | 16% | 37% |
| Retailers and marketplaces | 17% | 16% |
| Forums, social and video | 20% | 8% |

Google's first page is mostly review articles, Reddit threads and Amazon. AI Mode cites far more brand stores and far fewer forums. Reddit appeared on Google's first page for 17 of 19 questions, yet AI Mode cited it in only 4 answers.

## AI Mode answers with products, not pages

For 11 of the 19 questions, the AI Mode answer was a set of product listings: a product name, a price, a star rating with a review count, and the seller. These answers cited few pages or none. One cited none at all.

| Product listing detail | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Product listings shown across 11 answers | 60 |
| Sold by the brand's own store | 27 of 60 (45%) |
| Sold by a retailer or marketplace | 33 of 60 (55%) |
| Showing a star rating and review count | 57 of 60 |

Google says its AI shopping experiences rely on product data in Merchant Center and the Shopping Graph built from it, along with reviews and ratings. That fits what I saw. For these questions, the deciding factor wasn't a ranking article. It was whether the product was in Google's product data with a price, a rating and the details that match the question.

The listings weren't only big brands either. Among them were a tallow cream with 59 reviews, a fresh dog food bundle with 23 reviews and a pair of leggings with 20 reviews, all sold from the brand's own store.

## By product type

| Product type | Questions | Answers built from product listings | Google's top result cited in AI Mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Supplements | 4 | 0 | 2 |
| Skincare | 4 | 4 | 1 |
| Pet | 4 | 2 | 2 |
| Food and drink | 4 | 2 | 1 |
| Apparel | 3 | 3 | 1 |

Supplement questions got article style answers that cited reviews and brand pages. Skincare and apparel questions got product listings every time.

## What to do with this

1. **Get your products into Google's product data properly.** Connect your store to Merchant Center through the Google and YouTube app in Shopify, and fill in every attribute that matches how people shop: size, material, skin type, flavor, who it's for. Missing details are how products get skipped.
2. **Make your reviews visible to Google.** 57 of 60 listings showed a star rating and review count. Make sure your reviews reach Google, through your reviews app's Google integration and Product review structured data.
3. **Sell from your own store, not only through retailers.** When a brand's own listing was the seller, the click went to the brand. 27 of 60 listings did that.
4. **Answer the exact questions on your own pages.** AI Mode cited brand pages more than twice as often as Google's first page showed them. Product pages and guides that plainly answer "which one is best for sensitive skin" or "is this good for flat feet" give it something to quote.
5. **Don't stop at ranking.** A first page ranking covers one search. Check AI Mode for the questions your customers actually ask, since only 40% of what it cited came from Google's first page.

## How this was done

On November 14, 2026, I ran 19 buyer questions across five product types (supplements, skincare, pet, food and drink, apparel) in Google search and in Google AI Mode, from one US English session. A planned set of home product questions wasn't completed because Google limited the session after 38 searches. For each question I recorded the organic results on Google's first page (8 to 10 per question, not counting ads or other features) and every outside site linked in the AI Mode answer. I compared the two at the domain level. Product listings were counted from the AI Mode answer text, with duplicate sale price lines removed. Each site was classified as a publisher or expert site, a brand store, a retailer or marketplace, or a forum, social or video site. AI answers change from run to run and by location, so treat this as a snapshot of one day, not a ranking. Brand and store names aren't published.
