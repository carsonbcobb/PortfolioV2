**By Carson Cobb. Data collected November 4 to 11, 2026.**

On Google, a shopping search shows ten results, ads and a carousel. In an AI answer, a shopper sees a short list. I counted exactly how short across 144 answers: 48 shopping questions asked in ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode.

## The short version

- **ChatGPT names a median of 4 brands per answer.** Never more than 8.
- **Gemini and Google AI Mode name a median of 5.**
- **A typical question has 8 brands in play** across the three engines.
- **Only 2 are named by all three.** And 5 of 48 questions had no brand in common at all.
- **The lists barely repeat.** ChatGPT named 193 different brands, and only 8 appeared in more than one answer.

## Brands named per answer

| Engine | Median | Average | Most in one answer | Answers with no brand | Different brands across 48 answers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ChatGPT | 4 | 4.2 | 8 | 3 | 193 |
| Gemini | 5 | 5.5 | 12 | 3 | 254 |
| Google AI Mode | 5 | 5.1 | 11 | 3 | 230 |

## How much the engines agree

| For each question | Median |
| --- | --- |
| Brands named by any engine | 8 |
| Brands named by all three | 2 |

Of the 419 brand and question pairs in the data, 222 (53%) were named by only one engine, 101 by two and 96 (23%) by all three.

## What it means

Four slots is a much smaller shelf than a page of search results. And because the lists barely repeat from question to question, being named for one question says little about the next. A brand that shows up for "best gluten free granola" can be absent for "best granola for kids".

That's why I test many question variations, not one. I covered how much wording changes the answer in [how question wording changes AI picks](/reports/how-question-wording-changes-ai-recommendations), and how stable the top pick is in [how often AI recommendations change](/reports/how-often-ai-recommendations-change).

## What to do with this

1. **Write down the 20 questions your buyers actually ask.** By need, not by your product names.
2. **Ask all three engines.** Agreement across them is rare, so one engine's answer isn't the picture.
3. **Track which questions you're in, not a single score.**
4. **Go after the questions you're closest to.** Where you're named by one engine, find what the source behind it says, and earn the same coverage elsewhere.

## How this was done

- **Questions:** 48 shopping questions, eight each in supplements, beauty, food and drink, pet, home and apparel, written the way shoppers ask.
- **Engines:** ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode, each in a fresh session, November 4 to 11, 2026.
- **Counting:** every brand named in each answer was coded by hand and counted once per answer.
- **Limits:** one run per question per engine. Answers vary between runs, so treat the counts as typical, not fixed.

No store or brand is named in this report.
