Every agency now sells GEO, generative engine optimization. Some say it replaces SEO. Some say it's SEO with a new name. For online stores, the data says something in between: the foundation is shared, and what wins is different.

## Where they differ

| | Classic SEO | AI answers (GEO) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What the shopper sees | Ten results, ads, a product carousel | A short list: a median of 4 brands in ChatGPT, 5 in Gemini and AI Mode |
| What decides inclusion | Relevance, links, page quality | What independent sources say, plus specific reasons that match the shopper's need |
| Overlap with Google's first page | Is Google's first page | Only 40% of sites Google AI Mode cited ranked on page one for the same question |
| Role of Reddit | Top Google result for 11 of 19 shopping questions I checked | Cited by AI Mode for 4 of 19, shown by ChatGPT once in 48 |
| Brand stores | 16% of first page results | 37% of sites AI Mode cited |
| Stability | Rankings move slowly | The top pick is stable, but rewording a question changes the list a lot |
| Measurement | Rankings and clicks | Mentions across many question variants, plus AI referrals that undercount |

Sources: [Google rankings vs AI Mode](/reports/google-rankings-vs-ai-mode-2026), [how many brands AI recommends](/reports/how-many-brands-ai-recommends-2026), [sources ChatGPT cites](/reports/sources-chatgpt-cites-2026), [how often AI picks change](/reports/how-often-ai-recommendations-change).

## Where they're the same

Both need pages machines can read. In my scan of 617 Shopify brand stores, the basics were mostly in place: 86% of product pages had Product structured data, 99% of stores allowed the major AI crawlers, and canonical tags were near universal. Both reward product pages that answer real questions with specific facts.

Both also punish messy data. Duplicate product blocks (21% of product pages), missing identifiers (77% have no GTIN in structured data) and policies that only exist in paragraphs hurt Google Shopping and AI answers alike.

## What's extra for AI

1. **Independent coverage.** ChatGPT showed sources in 45 of 48 shopping answers, led by review and testing sites. That's PR and product seeding as much as SEO.
2. **Who it's for.** AI labels picks by need and asks about the shopper. Only 38% of product pages I scanned say who the product is for.
3. **Consistent facts everywhere.** AI pulls ratings and specs from many places, and gets them wrong when they disagree.
4. **Testing many questions.** One keyword isn't one answer. Wording changes the list.

## What's extra for SEO

Links, site structure, internal linking and the click. AI answers often satisfy the shopper before a click, and those that click arrive later in the decision. Shopify said AI referred shoppers convert 64% better than organic search in Q3 2026 ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/shopify-revenue-gains-32-to-3-7bn-as-ai-referred-orders-rise-3-3x/)).

## The practical answer

Run one plan. Fix the shared foundation, write product pages that state facts and fit, then add the AI specific work: coverage, consistency and testing across ChatGPT, Gemini and AI Mode.
