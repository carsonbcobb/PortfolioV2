**By Carson Cobb. Data checked November 2026.**

When ChatGPT recommends a product, it usually shows where the information came from: small source labels next to the claims, and links out to stores. Those sources are the closest thing to a map of what decides who gets recommended. If a review site, a magazine or a veterinary resource is behind most answers in your category, that's where your brand needs to be.

I recorded every visible source across 48 buyer questions in six categories.

## The short version

- **45 of 48 answers showed a source or a store link.** ChatGPT almost never recommends products without showing where it looked.
- **Review and testing sites lead.** They appeared in 35% of answers, ahead of magazines and publishers at 33%.
- **Brand stores are part of the answer.** 29% of answers linked straight to the recommended brands' own stores.
- **In health categories, experts outrank reviewers.** Medical, veterinary and research sources appeared in 29% of answers, nearly all in pet, supplements and beauty.
- **Reddit was rarely shown.** It appeared as a visible source once. Amazon never appeared as a visible source.

## Which kinds of sources ChatGPT showed

| Source type | Examples | Answers showing it | Categories |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Independent review or testing site | Consumer Reports, Sleep Foundation, Treeline Review, RunRepeat, ConsumerLab, niche testing blogs | 35% | All six |
| Magazine or publisher | Bon Appétit, Vogue, Glamour, Good Housekeeping, WIRED, Food & Wine, Healthline | 33% | Five of six |
| Link to the brand's own store | "Shop" and "Official website" links | 29% | Five of six |
| Medical, veterinary or research source | VCA Animal Hospitals, PetMD, PubMed, American Academy of Dermatology, Cleveland Clinic | 29% | Beauty, home, pet, supplements |
| Retailer | REI, iHerb | 10% | Apparel, food |
| Standards or certification body | NSF, Veterinary Oral Health Council, American Cleaning Institute, NRDC | 8% | Home, pet, supplements |
| Forum | Reddit | 2% | Food |

ChatGPT showed 56 different named sources across the 48 answers. Most appeared only once. The source that matters in your category is usually one or two specific sites, not "the internet."

## What each category leans on

- **Apparel:** outdoor and running testers (Treeline Review, GearJunkie, RunRepeat, REI) and fashion magazines. Sustainability questions pulled from sustainability rating sites.
- **Beauty and skincare:** fashion and beauty magazines for makeup, dermatology sources for skin concerns.
- **Food and beverage:** food publishers like Bon Appétit and Food & Wine, specialty review sites, and specialty retailers.
- **Home:** Consumer Reports, Sleep Foundation, design magazines and environmental groups.
- **Pet:** veterinary sources almost every time, plus the Veterinary Oral Health Council for dental products.
- **Supplements:** certification bodies like NSF, research databases and niche testing sites.

## Brand stores: where AI shoppers land

In 14 of 48 answers, ChatGPT linked each recommended brand's own store directly. For fresh dog food it labeled five brands' sites as "Official website." For kids vitamins it cited a brand website as a source.

That fits Shopify's own data: about 55% of AI referred sessions start on a product page. When ChatGPT sends a shopper to your store, your product page has to confirm what the answer said. If the price, the rating or the claim doesn't match, the visit doesn't convert.

## What about Reddit and Amazon?

Reddit is often described as the main source behind AI shopping answers. In this test it appeared as a visible source in 1 of 48 answers, for loose leaf tea. Amazon never appeared as a visible source, though a few product cards listed retailer prices.

There's an important limit here. ChatGPT often shows only the first source for a claim and hides the rest behind a count, like "+2." Across the 48 answers, 71 additional sources were hidden this way. Reddit and Amazon may be among them. What this test shows is what ChatGPT chose to show shoppers first.

## What to do with this

1. **Find your category's two or three sources.** Ask ChatGPT five buyer questions in your category and write down every source label it shows.
2. **Get onto those sources.** Send product to the review and testing sites, pitch the publishers' gift guides and roundups, and make sure experts in health categories know your product.
3. **Earn a certification if your category uses one.** In supplements and pet dental care, ChatGPT pointed to certification bodies by name.
4. **Treat your product page as the landing page for AI.** It's where the shopper goes next. See the [AI Readiness Index](/reports/shopify-ai-readiness-index-2026).

## How this was done

- **Questions:** the same 48 buyer questions as [Who ChatGPT recommends](/reports/who-chatgpt-recommends-2026), 8 per category, asked in new temporary chats in November 2026 on a paid ChatGPT plan with default settings.
- **Sources:** every visible source label in each answer, plus links to brand stores. Labels were grouped by type by hand. Each type counts once per answer.
- **Limits:** only the first source of each citation is visible without opening it, so these numbers show what ChatGPT displays first, not every page it read. 71 further sources were hidden behind counts. Answers can vary between conversations and by location.
