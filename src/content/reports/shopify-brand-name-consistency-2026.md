**By Carson Cobb. Data collected November 16, 2026.**

To recommend your brand, AI has to recognize it. That sounds obvious, but your brand name lives in several places on a Shopify store: the site name in your settings, the Organization data your theme publishes, and the vendor field on every product, which most themes publish as the product's brand.

If those disagree, you're asking AI to work out that three names are one brand. I compared them on 107 Shopify brand stores.

## The short version

- **1 in 10 stores publish more than one version of their own name.** 10 of 96 stores I could compare.
- **The common variations:** a country added ("USA", "US"), the product type added, a different legal or parent name, or an internal code in the vendor field.
- **25% don't state their name in Organization data.** 27 of 107 homepages.
- **13 product pages had no brand in their product data.**
- **9 stores had no site name set** for link previews.

## What I compared

| Where the name appears | What sets it on Shopify |
| --- | --- |
| Site name (`og:site_name`) | Store name in settings, used by the theme |
| Organization structured data | The theme, sometimes an app |
| Brand in Product structured data | Usually the product's vendor field |

## What I found

| | Stores |
| --- | --- |
| Stores with at least two of the three to compare | 96 |
| Same name in all places (allowing for case and symbols) | 86 |
| Two or more different versions of their own name | 10 |
| No Organization name in structured data | 27 of 107 |
| No brand in Product structured data | 13 of 104 product pages |
| No site name set | 9 of 107 |

The 10 inconsistent stores, described without naming them:

- A country suffix on the site name but not the brand ("Brand USA" vs "Brand").
- The product type added in one place ("Brand Pajamas" vs "Brand").
- A longer company or parent name in the product data than on the site.
- A slogan or hashtag attached to the site name.
- An internal vendor code published as the brand in product data.

A few other differences weren't counted, because they're legitimate: stores that sell other brands' products correctly listed those brands, and one store listed a sub brand for a product line.

## Why this matters

AI builds its picture of a brand by connecting mentions across sources. Your site, your product data, retailer listings, review sites and articles all need to point to the same entity. "Brand USA" on the site, "Brand Co." on Amazon and "BRAND" in product data look like a small detail to a person and like three candidates to a machine.

The internal code case is the clearest problem: the product data is telling search engines and AI that the brand is a word the shopper will never search for.

For the catalog fields that carry your brand name to AI shopping tools, see [product type, tags and vendor data](/reports/shopify-product-type-tags-vendor-data-2026).

## What to do with this

1. **Choose one exact brand name.** The form you want shoppers and AI to use.
2. **Set the store name to it.** In Shopify settings, and check your theme uses it for the site name.
3. **Fix the vendor field on every product.** Bulk edit in Shopify admin so every product you make has the same brand.
4. **Add or check Organization data on the homepage.** Name, logo and links to your official profiles.
5. **Match it outside your site.** Amazon, retailers, Google Business Profile and social bios. See [off site signals](/reports/shopify-off-site-signals-2026).

## How this was done

- **Stores:** 107 Shopify brand stores, 17 to 18 in each of six product areas (supplements, beauty, food and drink, pet, home, apparel), drawn at random from my dataset of 620 Shopify brand stores. Client stores were excluded.
- **Names:** the `og:site_name` tag and Organization structured data on each homepage, and the brand in Product structured data on one product page per store, read November 16, 2026.
- **Comparison:** names were compared after removing case, spaces, punctuation, trademark symbols and words like "Inc" and "Co". Each difference was then checked by hand, and differences that weren't about the store's own name (multi brand retailers, a sub brand) were excluded.
- **Limits:** three places on the site only. Retailers, review sites and social profiles weren't compared.

No store or brand is named in this report.
