**By Carson Cobb. Data collected November 16, 2026.**

Shoppers buying something they'll put on their skin, feed their dog or give their child want to know who's behind it. Google and AI ask a similar question in their own way: is this a real, trustworthy brand?

The About page is where most brands answer it. I checked 107 Shopify brand stores for an About or Our Story page and whether it said who started the brand.

## The short version

- **82% have an About page.** 88 of 107.
- **Only about half of those mention a founder.** 46 of 88 (52%).
- **That means 57% of stores never say who's behind the brand on an About page.** 61 of 107.
- **The About page is one of the most common pages on Shopify sites**, alongside the FAQ.

## What I found

| | Stores |
| --- | --- |
| Have an About or Our Story page | 88 of 107 (82%) |
| About page mentions a founder or how the brand was founded | 46 of 88 (52%) |
| No About page, or one with no founder | 61 of 107 (57%) |

## Mission statements don't answer the question

Most About pages without a founder had a mission statement: clean ingredients, better materials, a healthier planet. Those are fine, but every competitor says them too. They don't answer "who are you and why should I trust you?"

The pages that do answer it are specific: who started it, what problem they had, where the product is made, which experts are involved, how long they've been doing it. Specifics are what shoppers believe, and what AI can repeat. In my research, brand reputation and history came up as a reason in 10 to 18 of 48 AI answers per tool, and experts like vets and dermatologists in 9 to 16. See [why AI recommends a product](/reports/why-ai-recommends-products-reasons-2026).

## Why it matters more for some products

Google's guidance asks site owners to make it clear who is behind their content, and says its systems give even more weight to experience, expertise and trust on topics that could affect people's health, financial stability or safety ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)). Supplements, baby products and pet health are squarely in that group. A clear About page won't rank you on its own, but its absence leaves a gap in exactly the product types where trust decides the sale.

## What to do with this

1. **Name the people.** Founder, co founders, and any experts involved, with a photo and a sentence on why they're qualified.
2. **Tell the origin in specifics.** The problem, the year, the first product, what changed.
3. **Add the facts.** Where products are made, years in business, customers served, certifications.
4. **Link the proof.** Press coverage, review sites and a press page. See [social and press signals](/reports/shopify-off-site-signals-2026).
5. **Connect it in structured data.** Organization data on your homepage with your name, logo, founding date and official profiles.
6. **Link to it from product pages.** A short "Who we are" line near the product details, for the shopper deciding whether to trust you.

## How this was done

- **Stores:** 107 Shopify brand stores, 17 to 18 in each of six product areas (supplements, beauty, food and drink, pet, home, apparel), drawn at random from my dataset of 620 Shopify brand stores. Client stores were excluded.
- **About pages:** the first page in each store's pages sitemap with "about" or "our story" in the URL, fetched November 16, 2026.
- **Founder:** the page text mentioned "founder", "co founder" or "founded".
- **Limits:** stores that tell their story on the homepage or a page with a different URL weren't counted. Mentioning a founder doesn't mean naming one; this measures whether the page talks about who started the brand at all.

No store or brand is named in this report.
