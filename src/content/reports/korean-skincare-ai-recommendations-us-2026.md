**By Carson Cobb. Data collected November 23 and 24, 2026.**


## Key findings

- **Four brands own the answers.** COSRX, Beauty of Joseon, Anua and Round Lab appeared in 36 of 40 answers (90%). COSRX alone appeared in 20 and Beauty of Joseon in 19.
- **AI names about 4 brands per answer.** The median was 4 in both ChatGPT and Google AI Mode.
- **40 brands got named at least once, but 13 of them only once.** The long tail is real but thin.
- **The two engines mostly agree.** Every one of the 20 questions had at least one brand named by both ChatGPT and AI Mode, and the average overlap between their brand lists was 41%.
- **Rewording the question kept 58% of the brands.** When we asked 5 questions again in different words, 11 of the original 19 brands came back. The sunscreen answer kept all 3 brands. The moisturizer answer kept only 1 of 4.
- **The brand's own store got the click on only 1 in 4 product cards.** Of 92 product cards, the brand's own store was the seller on 23 (25%). Korean beauty resellers had 39 (42%), US retailers like Target, Ulta and Sephora had 22 (24%), and the rest went to Olive Young and marketplaces.
- **Google AI Mode leaned on Reddit.** Across all 25 AI Mode answers, Reddit threads were 20 of the 39 sources shown on screen, and appeared in 17 of the 20 answers that showed sources.
- **ChatGPT leaned on Ulta.** ChatGPT cited Ulta in 13 of 20 answers, more than any other site. Allure was the publication it cited most (7 of 20 answers).
- **Sunscreen answers warned about US versions.** Both engines told shoppers that some Korean sunscreens sell a different formula in the US, and to check which version they're buying.

The pattern matches what we see in every product area we test: AI gives a short list, and the same few names fill it. A shopper asking about toner pads, retinol, sheet masks or pimple patches gets different products, but mostly from the same brands.

## Do ChatGPT and Google AI Mode recommend the same brands?

Mostly, yes. Both engines named at least one of the same brands on all 20 questions. On average, 41% of the brands named across both answers to a question were named by both. Sometimes they matched almost exactly: asked for a gentle Korean retinol for beginners, both engines showed the same three products from Innisfree, Haruharu Wonder and Dr. Althea, at the same prices.

Where they split, it was usually on the third or fourth pick, not the first. That's where a brand outside the top four has the best chance to get in.

## Does the answer change if a shopper words the question differently?

Somewhat. We asked 5 of the questions a second time in Google AI Mode, worded the way a different shopper might ask. 11 of the 19 brands from the first answers came back (58%).

| Question | Brands kept |
|---|---|
| Sunscreen for combination skin | 3 of 3 |
| Retinol for beginners | 3 of 4 |
| Toner pads for oily skin | 2 of 4 |
| Gentle vitamin C serum | 2 of 4 |
| Lightweight winter moisturizer | 1 of 4 |

The leaders held. The picks below them moved. For a brand that isn't in the top four, that's the opening: the third and fourth spots are still up for grabs, and they change with how the shopper asks.

## Where does AI send US shoppers to buy?

This is the finding that matters most for an international brand. When AI showed product cards with a price and a seller, the brand's own store was the seller on only 23 of 92 cards (25%).

| Seller on the product card | Cards | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Korean beauty resellers (YesStyle, Stylevana, Stylekorean and smaller import shops) | 39 | 42% |
| Brand's own store (for example Anua US, Mediheal US, SKIN1004.com, COSRX, Round Lab) | 23 | 25% |
| US retailers (Target, Ulta, Sephora, Walmart, Kohl's, Nordstrom and others) | 22 | 24% |
| Marketplaces (weee!, eBay, FragranceNet) | 5 | 5% |
| Olive Young | 3 | 3% |

When a reseller holds the card, the reseller sets the price the shopper sees, owns the customer and keeps the margin. One answer shows what's possible when a brand gets this right: asked a second time about toner pads, Google AI Mode showed 4 product cards and 3 of them were sold by the brand's own US store.

## What sources does AI use for Korean skincare?

**Google AI Mode:** Reddit dominated what it showed on screen. Across all 25 AI Mode answers, 20 of the 39 visible sources were Reddit threads, mostly from r/AsianBeauty and r/KoreanBeauty, and Reddit appeared in 17 of the 20 answers that showed sources. YouTube reviewers, Allure, Glamour and a few brand sites made up the rest.

**ChatGPT:** It cited Ulta in 13 of 20 answers, more than any other site, and cited Allure more than any other publication. It also linked to brand sites when they had the right page, such as Beauty of Joseon's own site and a Korean brand's US site.

The two engines reward different things. AI Mode listens to what shoppers say in communities. ChatGPT leans on large US retailers and beauty editors.

## What this means for a Korean or international brand selling into the US

1. **Run a US store with US prices, shipping and stock, and feed it to Google.** If AI can't find your US offer, it shows a reseller's. A clean product feed and product structured data with US prices give AI your offer to show.
2. **Get into the US retailers AI already trusts.** ChatGPT cited Ulta in 13 of 20 answers. A retailer listing helps you get named, even when your own store is set up well.
3. **Answer US shopper questions on your product pages.** Shoppers asked about US shipping, white cast on darker skin, sensitive skin, first time use and whether a sunscreen is the US formula. A page that answers those plainly gives AI something to quote.
4. **Be part of the conversation on Reddit and with beauty editors.** AI Mode showed Reddit more than any other source. Allure, Glamour and Vogue shaped ChatGPT's answers. Brands that show up there show up in answers.
5. **Go after the third and fourth spots first.** The top two spots barely moved. The lower spots changed with the wording, so that's where a growing brand can win soonest.

## How we ran this test

- **Questions:** 20 questions about Korean skincare, written the way real US shoppers ask, with skin type, age or a US shipping need (for example: "I have dark skin and hate white cast. Which Korean sunscreen won't leave a white cast?"). Full list in [kbeauty_answers.csv](/data/reports/korean-skincare-ai-recommendations-us-2026/kbeauty_answers.csv).
- **Engines:** Google AI Mode (US and English settings, signed in to a Google account) and ChatGPT (logged out, default model). Each question asked once per engine, plus 5 questions asked again in AI Mode with different wording.
- **Dates:** November 23 and 24, 2026.
- **What we recorded:** brands named in each answer, product cards (product, price and seller), and the sources each engine showed. Brands were matched against a list of more than 100 skincare brand names and checked against each answer's product cards.
- **Limits:** This is one snapshot. AI answers change over time and from person to person. Google AI Mode shows only a few sources before "Show all," so source counts cover what was visible on screen. ChatGPT answers come from a logged out session. La Roche-Posay was the only non-Korean brand recommended, in one answer.
