**By Carson Cobb. Data collected November 23 and 24, 2026.**


## Key findings

- **Four brands own the answers.** COSRX, Beauty of Joseon, Anua and Round Lab appeared in 56 of 60 answers (93%). COSRX alone appeared in 35.
- **All three AIs agree on the leaders.** On every one of the 20 questions, at least one brand was named by ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode alike.
- **Gemini shows the longest list.** Gemini named a median of 7 brands per answer, against 4 for ChatGPT and Google AI Mode, and 33 brands appeared only in Gemini's answers. If you're a smaller brand, Gemini is where you're most likely to get a first mention.
- **The brand's own store got the click on just 15% of product cards.** Of 241 product cards across the three AIs, the brand's own store was the seller on 37. Korean beauty resellers had 56%. In Gemini, the brand's own store got just 9%.
- **Asking about fakes changes who sells.** When the shopper asked which brands to trust and how to avoid fakes, Gemini sent 6 of 8 product cards to the brand's own store.
- **Rewording the question kept 58% of the brands.** Asked a second time in different words in Google AI Mode, 11 of the original 19 brands came back. The leaders held. The lower spots moved.
- **Google AI Mode leaned on Reddit. ChatGPT leaned on Ulta.** Reddit threads were 20 of the 39 sources AI Mode showed on screen. ChatGPT cited Ulta in 13 of 20 answers.
- **Sunscreen answers warned about US versions.** All three AIs told shoppers that some Korean sunscreens sell a different formula in the US.

The pattern matches every product area we test: AI gives a short list, and the same few names fill it. A shopper asking about toner pads, retinol, sheet masks or pimple patches gets different products, but mostly from the same brands.

## Do ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode recommend the same brands?

At the top, yes. All 20 questions had at least one brand that all three named. Below the top, they split: of the 73 brands named at least once, 25 were named by all three AIs and 41 by only one.

Gemini is the outlier. Its answers came with more product cards (a median of 7 brands per answer), and it reached further into the long tail, naming 33 brands no other AI mentioned, from Centellian24 and Real Barrier to TIAM and Blithe. ChatGPT and Google AI Mode stuck closer to the best known names, and sometimes matched almost exactly: asked for a gentle Korean retinol for beginners, both showed the same three products at the same prices.

## Does the answer change if a shopper words the question differently?

Somewhat. We asked 5 of the questions a second time in Google AI Mode, worded the way a different shopper might ask. 11 of the 19 brands from the first answers came back (58%).

| Question | Brands kept |
|---|---|
| Sunscreen for combination skin | 3 of 3 |
| Retinol for beginners | 3 of 4 |
| Toner pads for oily skin | 2 of 4 |
| Gentle vitamin C serum | 2 of 4 |
| Lightweight winter moisturizer | 1 of 4 |

The leaders held. The picks below them moved. For a brand outside the top four, the third and fourth spots are still up for grabs, and they change with how the shopper asks.

## Where does AI send US shoppers to buy?

This is the finding that matters most for an international brand. Across 241 product cards with a price and a seller, the brand's own store was the seller on only 37 (15%).

| Seller on the product card | Cards | Share of all cards |
|---|---|---|
| Korean beauty resellers (YesStyle, Stylevana, Stylekorean and many smaller import shops) | 136 | 56% |
| US retailers (Sephora, Ulta, Target, Nordstrom, Macy's, CVS and others) | 43 | 18% |
| Brand's own store (for example Anua US, Mediheal US, SKIN1004.com, COSRX, Laneige, Round Lab) | 37 | 15% |
| Olive Young | 10 | 4% |
| Marketplaces (weee!, Yami, eBay and others) | 10 | 4% |
| No seller shown | 5 | 2% |

| AI | Product cards | Brand's own store |
|---|---|---|
| Google AI Mode | 57 | 14 (25%) |
| ChatGPT | 35 | 9 (26%) |
| Gemini | 149 | 14 (9%) |

When a reseller holds the card, the reseller sets the price the shopper sees, owns the customer and keeps the margin. Two answers show what's possible when a brand gets this right. Asked a second time about toner pads, Google AI Mode showed 4 product cards and 3 were sold by the brand's own US store. And when the shopper asked Gemini which brands to trust without buying fakes, 6 of 8 cards went to the brand's own store.

## What sources does AI use for Korean skincare?

**Google AI Mode:** Reddit dominated what it showed on screen. Across 25 AI Mode answers, 20 of the 39 visible sources were Reddit threads, mostly from r/AsianBeauty and r/KoreanBeauty, and Reddit appeared in 17 of the 20 answers that showed sources. YouTube reviewers, Allure, Glamour and a few brand sites made up the rest.

**ChatGPT:** It cited Ulta in 13 of 20 answers, more than any other site, and cited Allure more than any other publication (7 of 20). It also linked to brand sites when they had the right page.

**Gemini:** Its answers were built around product cards that appear to come from Google's shopping results, with short notes from Korean beauty retailers' blogs and review sites under each product.

The three AIs reward different things. AI Mode listens to what shoppers say in communities. ChatGPT leans on large US retailers and beauty editors. Gemini leans on whoever has a product listing in Google's shopping results, which is why resellers dominate its cards.

## What this means for a Korean or international brand selling into the US

1. **Run a US store with US prices, shipping and stock, and feed it to Google.** Gemini and AI Mode build product cards from Google's shopping results. If your US offer isn't there with a clean product feed, a reseller's listing takes the card and sets the price.
2. **Get into the US retailers AI already trusts.** ChatGPT cited Ulta in 13 of 20 answers. A retailer listing helps you get named even when your own store is set up well.
3. **Answer US shopper questions on your product pages.** Shoppers asked about US shipping, white cast on darker skin, sensitive skin, first time use and whether a sunscreen is the US formula. A page that answers those plainly gives AI something to quote.
4. **Make authenticity easy to confirm.** When shoppers worry about fakes, AI sends them to official stores. Say clearly on your site where your products are sold officially in the US.
5. **Be part of the conversation on Reddit and with beauty editors.** AI Mode showed Reddit more than any other source. Allure, Glamour and Vogue shaped ChatGPT's answers.
6. **Go after the third and fourth spots, and start with Gemini.** The top spots barely moved, but the lower spots changed with the wording, and Gemini named far more brands. That's where a growing brand can win soonest.

## How we ran this test

- **Questions:** 20 questions about Korean skincare, written the way real US shoppers ask, with skin type, age or a US shipping need (for example: "I have dark skin and hate white cast. Which Korean sunscreen won't leave a white cast?"). Full list in [kbeauty_answers.csv](/data/reports/korean-skincare-ai-recommendations-us-2026/kbeauty_answers.csv).
- **AIs:** Google AI Mode (US and English settings, signed in to a Google account), Gemini (signed in, default model) and ChatGPT (logged out, default model). Each question asked once per AI, plus 5 questions asked again in AI Mode with different wording. 65 answers in total.
- **Dates:** November 23 and 24, 2026.
- **What we recorded:** brands named in each answer, product cards (product, price and seller), and the sources each AI showed. For ChatGPT and Google AI Mode, brands were matched against a list of more than 100 skincare brand names and checked against each answer's product cards. For Gemini, brands were taken from its product cards and checked against the answer text.
- **Seller types:** a card counts as the brand's own store when the seller shown is the brand or its official US site.
- **Limits:** This is one snapshot. AI answers change over time and from person to person. Google AI Mode shows only a few sources before "Show all," so source counts cover what was visible on screen. A few brands named were not Korean, or were US brands inspired by Korean skincare. They're included in the counts as the AIs named them.
