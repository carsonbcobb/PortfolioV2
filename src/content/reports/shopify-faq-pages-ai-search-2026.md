**By Carson Cobb. Data collected November 16, 2026.**

A good FAQ page answers the questions that stop a sale: will it arrive in time, can I return it, is it safe for me, how do I use it. Now it also answers those questions for AI, which reads store pages to respond to shoppers directly.

I found the FAQ page of 83 Shopify brand stores and checked how many questions it answered and which topics it covered.

## The short version

- **79% of stores have a FAQ page.** 83 of 105.
- **The median FAQ has about 22 questions.** 20 had fewer than 10.
- **Shipping and returns are covered by 95%.**
- **Safety is the most skipped topic.** 35% of FAQ pages don't cover allergies, sensitivities, pregnancy or side effects.
- **Only 23% use FAQ structured data.** 19 of 83.

## What FAQ pages cover

| Topic | FAQ pages | Share of 83 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shipping | 79 | 95% |
| Returns and refunds | 79 | 95% |
| How to use, dosage, sizing or fit | 65 | 78% |
| Subscriptions | 63 | 76% |
| Ingredients, materials or what it's made of | 60 | 72% |
| Safety (allergies, sensitivities, pregnancy, side effects) | 54 | 65% |
| International orders | 45 | 54% |

For every page type brands publish, see [which pages Shopify brands publish](/reports/shopify-store-pages-benchmarks-2026).

## The gap is the product, not the store

FAQ pages are strong on store logistics: shipping, returns, subscriptions. They're weaker on the product itself: is it safe for me, what's in it, how do I use it, which one should I pick.

Those product questions are exactly what shoppers ask AI. In my research, ChatGPT asked follow up questions about skin type, pet size, age and the result the shopper wanted before choosing. See [the follow up questions ChatGPT asks shoppers](/reports/chatgpt-follow-up-questions-shoppers-2026). A FAQ that only covers shipping can't help with any of that.

## FAQ structured data

19 of 83 FAQ pages (23%) added FAQ structured data. Google restricted FAQ rich results to a small set of authoritative sites in 2023 ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes)), so for most stores it won't change how the page looks in search. The questions and answers on the page are what matter.

## What to do with this

1. **Pull your real questions.** Support tickets, chat logs, reviews and the questions AI asks shoppers about your product type.
2. **Add the product questions.** Who it's for, who it's not for, ingredients or materials, how to use it, how long it lasts, how to choose between your products.
3. **Answer safety questions plainly.** Allergies, sensitivities, pregnancy, kids, pets, with a clear answer or a clear "ask your doctor".
4. **Answer in the first sentence.** "Yes, it's safe for dogs over 12 weeks." Then the detail.
5. **Put product specific answers on product pages too.** The FAQ page is for store wide questions; the product page should answer its own. See [do product pages answer AI shopper questions](/reports/do-product-pages-answer-ai-shopper-questions).
6. **Keep answers in line with your policies.** The FAQ, the policy pages and the product pages should never disagree.

What shoppers and AI can find out about your products is part of [Close the Gap](/close-the-gap). The [AI Visibility Check](/ai-visibility-check) is a quick first look.

## How this was done

- **Stores:** 107 Shopify brand stores, 17 to 18 in each of six product areas (supplements, beauty, food and drink, pet, home, apparel), drawn at random from my dataset of 620 Shopify brand stores. Client stores were excluded. 105 had a readable pages sitemap.
- **FAQ pages:** the first page in each store's pages sitemap with "faq" or "frequently" in the URL, fetched November 16, 2026.
- **Questions:** estimated by counting question marks in the page text. Topics: keyword rules on the page text (for example "ship", "return", "subscri", "ingredient", "allerg", "pregnan").
- **Limits:** stores that keep their FAQ in a separate help center or app weren't counted as having a FAQ page. Topic detection checks whether a topic is mentioned, not how well it's answered.

No store or brand is named in this report.
