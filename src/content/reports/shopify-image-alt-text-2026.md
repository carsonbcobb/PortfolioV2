**By Carson Cobb. Data collected November 16, 2026.**

Alt text is the short description attached to an image. Shoppers using screen readers hear it. Google reads it to understand the image. AI tools that read product pages and product data use it too, because it's text that says what the picture shows.

It takes a few seconds per image, which is why most stores skip it. I checked the product images of 105 Shopify brand stores to see how many did.

## The short version

- **54% of product images had alt text.** 1,637 of 3,039.
- **35% of stores had none.** 37 of 105 stores had no alt text on any image I checked.
- **25% of stores had it on every image.** 26 of 105.
- **Almost no one just repeated the product name.** Only 2 images used the exact product title as alt text.

## What I found

| | Count |
| --- | --- |
| Stores checked | 105 |
| Product images checked | 3,039 |
| Images with alt text | 1,637 (54%) |
| Stores with alt text on every image | 26 (25%) |
| Stores with alt text on half or more | 48 (46%) |
| Stores with no alt text on any image | 37 (35%) |

63 of 105 stores were all or nothing: either every image I checked had alt text, or none did. That usually comes down to whether someone made it part of the process of adding a product. The other 42 stores had it on some images and not others.

## Why it matters

**Accessibility.** For a shopper using a screen reader, an image with no alt text is a blank. On a product page where the images show the size, the ingredients or how it's used, that's missing information.

**Google.** Alt text is one of the ways Google understands an image for Google Images and Shopping. A product image that says "Front of the 8 oz jar with ingredient list" can match more searches than one that says nothing.

**AI.** AI shopping tools read your product pages and your product data. Alt text is part of that data. It won't decide a recommendation on its own, but it's one more place your product facts are stated in words. See [product data for AI shopping](/reports/shopify-catalog-data-ai-shopping-2026).

Alt text is part of accessibility too. See [accessibility overlays on Shopify](/reports/shopify-accessibility-overlays-2026) for the overlay tools brands load.

## What good alt text looks like

Describe what the image shows, briefly:

- "Front of the 60 capsule bottle"
- "Supplement facts panel showing 500 mg magnesium per serving"
- "Model wearing the leggings in navy, size small, squatting"
- "Dog wearing the harness, front clip attached"

Avoid the same product name on every image, and avoid lists of keywords.

## What to do with this

1. **Start with your best sellers.** Add alt text to every image on your top 20 products.
2. **Describe the image, not the product.** What's in this specific picture: angle, variant, label, use.
3. **Prioritize images with information.** Ingredient panels, size charts and comparison images matter most, because they hold facts.
4. **Make it a step in product launches.** New products get alt text before they go live.
5. **Use bulk tools for big catalogs.** Shopify's admin works product by product; apps or CSV editing work for hundreds.

## How this was done

- **Stores:** 107 Shopify brand stores, 17 to 18 in each of six product areas (supplements, beauty, food and drink, pet, home, apparel), drawn at random from my dataset of 620 Shopify brand stores. Client stores were excluded. 105 returned product data.
- **Images:** for each store, the first 5 products in its public product list (gift cards excluded), and every image of those products, read from each product's public data file (`/products/handle.json`) on November 16, 2026. An image counted as having alt text when the alt field wasn't empty.
- **Limits:** 5 products per store. Stores often have better alt text on newer products than older ones, or the reverse. Quality of alt text wasn't scored beyond whether it existed and whether it only repeated the product title.

No store or brand is named in this report.
