**By Carson Cobb. Data collected November 4 and 5, 2026.**

When ChatGPT or Google's AI recommends a product, it gives a reason. "Third party tested." "Made with merino." "Best value per serving." Those reasons are what the shopper reads, and they tell you what the AI found convincing.

I took the 48 buyer questions from my [Who ChatGPT recommends](/reports/who-chatgpt-recommends-2026) research, asked in ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode, and counted the kinds of reasons and claims each AI gave.

## The short version

- **Value, materials and ingredients lead.** Each came up in 29 to 37 answers per AI.
- **Testing and editor reviews are a big reason for ChatGPT.** 24 of 48 answers.
- **Gemini and AI Mode quote certifications far more.** 23 and 25 answers, against 12 for ChatGPT.
- **Organic is the most repeated claim.** 16 Gemini answers, 14 AI Mode, 7 ChatGPT.
- **Experts get cited too.** Vets, dermatologists and doctors came up in 9 to 16 answers per AI.
- **Vague claims don't travel.** The reasons AI repeats are specific and checkable.

| Price or value | 34 | 32 | 31 |
| Ingredients or materials | 29 | 36 | 37 |

| Durability or performance | 21 | 22 | 25 |

| Customer reviews or ratings | 14 | 7 | 20 |
| Certifications | 12 | 23 | 25 |
| Brand reputation or history | 10 | 17 | 18 |
| Vets, dermatologists or doctors | 9 | 14 | 16 |

Out of 48 answers each. Several questions asked for sustainable or eco friendly products, so sustainability reasons were common across all three and aren't shown.

## What the pattern says

ChatGPT's reasons lean on outside judgment: who tested the product and what the editors said. That matches what it cites, mostly review and testing sites. See [the sources ChatGPT cites](/reports/sources-chatgpt-cites-2026).

Gemini and AI Mode lean more on product facts: what it's made of, what it's certified as, what experts say. Those facts come from product pages, product data and Google's shopping data, which is why complete product information matters more for Google's AI. See [product data for AI shopping](/reports/shopify-catalog-data-ai-shopping-2026).

Across all three, the reasons that show up are concrete. "Organic" can be checked. "Third party tested by NSF" can be checked. "Premium quality" and "made with love" never appeared as a reason in any answer. If your product page leads with feel good language and puts the facts in a tab, the AI has less to work with.

## What to do with this

1. **List your concrete claims.** Every certification, test, ingredient or material fact you can prove.
2. **Put them high on the product page, in plain words.** "USDA Organic. Third party tested by NSF." Not just a row of badges with no text.
3. **Add them to your product data.** Use descriptions, product attributes and your Merchant Center feed, so Google's AI can read them.
4. **Make sure the outside sources agree.** The certifier's directory, retailer listings and review sites should show the same claims.
5. **Get an expert on record.** A vet, dermatologist or dietitian who reviewed the product, named on the page, gives AI a reason it can cite.
6. **Cut the claims you can't support.** They don't help with AI, and they cost trust with shoppers.

## How this was done

- **Answers:** the 48 short buyer questions from [Who ChatGPT recommends](/reports/who-chatgpt-recommends-2026), 8 in each of six product areas, asked in ChatGPT on November 4, 2026 and in Gemini and Google AI Mode on November 5, 2026, each in a fresh session.
- **Reasons and claims:** an answer counted once for each reason or claim it mentioned anywhere in the text, detected with keyword rules (for example "third party", "clinically", "organic", "vet") and checked by hand.
- **Limits:** 48 questions per AI. The products asked about shape the reasons (questions about skincare bring more dermatologist mentions). This counts what AI said, not whether each claim was true.

No store or brand is named in this report.
