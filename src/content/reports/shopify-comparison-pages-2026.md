**By Carson Cobb. Data collected November 16, 2026.**

"Is this better than that?" is one of the most common things shoppers ask before buying, and now they ask it in ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode. When a brand doesn't answer the comparison on its own site, AI answers it from review sites, roundups and Reddit.

I checked the pages and blog articles of 105 Shopify brand stores for any content comparing their products with alternatives.

## The short version

- **Only 12% have a comparison page.** 13 of 105.
- **53% have any comparison content.** 56 of 105, counting blog articles.
- **47% have none anywhere.** 49 of 105.
- **Most stores have the trust pages.** 91% have FAQ or help pages and 90% an About page.
- **43% have a science, ingredients or testing page.** 45 of 105.

## What pages stores have

| Page type | Stores | Share of 105 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FAQ, help or support | 96 | 91% |
| About, our story or mission | 94 | 90% |
| Reviews or testimonials | 62 | 59% |
| Science, ingredients, research or testing | 45 | 43% |
| Press or media | 34 | 32% |
| Comparison (vs, compare, alternatives) | 13 | 12% |
| Any comparison page or comparison article | 56 | 53% |

The median store had 43 pages.

## Why comparisons are the gap that matters

Every store has an About page. Very few answer the question a shopper asks right before choosing. That's the moment the sale is decided, and most brands leave it to someone else.

When I tested how AI answers buying questions, ChatGPT's visible sources were led by review and testing sites and magazines, and Google AI Mode showed Reddit in 19 of 48 answers and YouTube in 18. See [where AI sends shoppers](/reports/where-ai-sends-shoppers-2026) and [the sources ChatGPT cites](/reports/sources-chatgpt-cites-2026). Those sources can be fair, but they're not written by the people who know the product best.

A comparison page on your own site doesn't guarantee AI quotes you. But without one, the comparison is decided entirely by others.

## What makes a good comparison page

- **Lead with the answer.** One or two sentences: who should pick which.
- **A table.** The facts that drive the decision, side by side: ingredients or materials, sizes, price per serving or per use, guarantee, shipping.
- **Who each is best for.** Including where the alternative is the better choice.
- **Specifics, not adjectives.** "20 g protein per serving" beats "more protein".
- **Current.** Update it when your product or the alternative changes.

You don't have to name a competitor. "Our collagen vs typical bovine collagen" or "Merino vs synthetic base layers" answers the question without it.

## What to do with this

1. **Find the comparisons shoppers make.** Ask ChatGPT "[your product] vs" and see what it suggests, and check Search Console for "vs" searches.
2. **Write the top three.** One page or article per comparison.
3. **Be fair.** A page that says the alternative wins on one point is more credible, to shoppers and AI, than one that wins on everything.
4. **Link from product pages.** A shopper on the product page who's comparing should find it in one click.
5. **Add a science or testing page if you sell on proof.** Only 43% of stores have one, and it's the evidence comparisons rely on.

## How this was done

- **Stores:** 107 Shopify brand stores, 17 to 18 in each of six product areas (supplements, beauty, food and drink, pet, home, apparel), drawn at random from my dataset of 620 Shopify brand stores. Client stores were excluded. 105 had a readable pages sitemap.
- **Pages:** every page in each store's pages sitemap, read November 16, 2026, classified by URL with keyword rules ("vs", "compare", "comparison", "alternative" for comparisons; "faq", "help", "support" for FAQ; and so on).
- **Articles:** blog article URLs from each store's article sitemaps, classified the same way, for the combined comparison figure. See [what Shopify brands publish on their blogs](/reports/shopify-blog-content-ai-search-2026).
- **Limits:** classifying by URL misses pages whose URL doesn't describe them, and comparison content inside a product page isn't counted, so these figures are likely a little low.

No store or brand is named in this report.
