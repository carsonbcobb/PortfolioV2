**By Carson Cobb. Data collected November 24, 2026.**


## Key findings

- **The two AIs mostly named different brands.** Across 20 questions, Google AI Mode named 125 brands and ChatGPT named 127. Only 50 brands were named by both. On the dog food question, they had no brands in common at all.
- **Google AI Mode matched the brand's site on 8 of 9 deals we checked.** ChatGPT matched on 4. It said it couldn't find or confirm a deal from AG1, Field Company and Salt & Stone, even though each sale was on the brand's homepage, and for AG1 and Field Company the code was in the page's HTML.
- **ChatGPT leaned on deal roundups.** It credited its deals to publisher roundups like Tom's Guide, Good Housekeeping, Sleep Foundation and Organic Authority. Brands that weren't in a roundup were often listed as "worth checking" with no deal.
- **Every ChatGPT answer came with an ad.** All 20 logged out ChatGPT answers showed a sponsored placement under the answer. 16 were from brands, and 14 of those 16 brands weren't named in ChatGPT's own answer. One ad showed a US shopper a price in British pounds.
- **Brands sold their own products more often than in our Korean skincare test.** The brand's own store was the seller on 39 of 71 Google AI Mode product cards (55%) and 18 of 44 ChatGPT cards (41%). In our [Korean skincare test](/reports/korean-skincare-ai-recommendations-us-2026), it was 15%.
- **AI shared discount codes, including ones that weren't the brand's.** Google AI Mode quoted 14 codes in 10 answers and ChatGPT 15 codes in 8 answers. One was an influencer's personal code. Another was a publisher's code that gave a different discount from the one on the brand's site.
- **One community thread can write the answer.** For candles, Google AI Mode credited 9 brand deals to a single Reddit thread in r/luxurycandles. ChatGPT said it checked its candle deals against one deal roundup.
- **Old pages still show up.** For supplements, the only source Google AI Mode showed was a 2025 article. For smart rings, one of its sources was a video from January 2025.

Mostly not. Both AIs named at least one brand in common on 19 of the 20 questions, but most of each answer was different.

The categories with well known leaders, like leggings and cookware, overlapped the most. Where the market is crowded and the leaders are less clear, like coffee, supplements and pet food, the two AIs gave almost entirely different lists. A shopper asking about coffee subscriptions got Atlas, Trade, Bean Box, Peet's and Carabello from Google AI Mode, and Sightglass, Verve, Partners, Blue Bottle, Atlas and Purity from ChatGPT.

## How accurate were the deals AI recommended?

We checked 9 deals against the brand's own homepage on the same day.

| Brand | Google AI Mode | ChatGPT | What the brand's site showed |
|---|---|---|---|
| AG1 | Right | Said no verified discount | 25% off first subscription order, code BFCM25 |
| Field Company | Right | No deal given | 25% off sitewide, code COOKFORKEEPS |
| Salt & Stone | Right | Said not confirmed | 20% off sitewide |
| Liquid I.V. | Right | Right | 30% off, code EARLY2026 |
| Native | Right | Right | 30% off orders of $35 or more, ends Dec 6 |
| Away | Right | Right | 25% off sitewide |
| Ritual | Right | Right | 40% off sitewide plus gifts |
| Bear | Right | Gave a publisher's 40% code | 35% off sitewide |
| Nuun | Called a bundle discount a Black Friday sale | No deal given | A bundle named "20% Off", no Black Friday sale on the homepage |

Google AI Mode found the brand's own offer more often. ChatGPT's deals matched the brand's site on 4 of the 5 it gave, but for the other 4 brands it gave no deal at all. On AG1, it told the shopper it found no verified Black Friday discount, while AG1's homepage showed the code BFCM25 in the banner, the page copy and the fine print. Field Company's code appeared in its page HTML four times.

The pattern across ChatGPT's answers: deals that appeared in a publisher roundup made it in. Deals that only lived on the brand's site often didn't.

Full details are in [black_friday_deal_checks.csv](/data/reports/black-friday-ai-deals-2026/black_friday_deal_checks.csv).

## Where do AI Black Friday deals come from?

**Google AI Mode** mixed publisher roundups (Forbes, Yahoo, Business Insider, USA Today, Vogue) with the brands' own pages and posts. It credited single deal lines to HexClad's and Field Company's sale pages, Casper's Instagram, Purple's site and a Greens First Facebook post. When it showed product cards, the price and seller came from Google's shopping results.

**ChatGPT** usually credited a roundup for each deal: Tom's Guide and Sleep Foundation for mattresses, Good Housekeeping and Architectural Digest for bedding, Organic Authority for cookware, a candle deal roundup for candles. It also pulled deals from an archive of brand marketing emails for two questions. It often added a short line that prices were listings it found, not confirmed Black Friday markdowns.

**Community threads matter more than you'd think.** One Reddit thread supplied most of Google AI Mode's candle answer: codes and discounts for Jonathan Adler, Apotheke, Hotel Lobby, Skandinavisk, Voluspa, Rituals and others.

## Which sellers did AI send shoppers to?

| AI | Product cards | Brand's own store | Retailers and marketplaces |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google AI Mode | 71 | 39 (55%) | 32 (45%) |
| ChatGPT | 44 | 18 (41%) | 26 (59%) |

Brands with a strong direct store did well: WalkingPad, FlexiSpot, lululemon, Columbia, Field Company, Baloo and Bearaby all sold their own products in the cards. Pet food, grooming kits and fitness trackers went mostly to retailers like Chewy, Petco, Walmart and Best Buy.

ChatGPT built some answers around retailers instead of brands. Asked about dog food deals, it listed Petco, Chewy and PetSmart offers first and named products only as examples.

## What about the ads in ChatGPT?

Every ChatGPT answer we got showed one sponsored placement under the answer, labeled "Ad."

| Advertiser type | Ads |
|---|---|
| Brands | 16 |
| Publishers and comparison sites | 3 |
| Retailers | 1 |

Most brand ads came from brands ChatGPT didn't name in its own answer: On for running shoes, WHOOP for fitness trackers, Pact for sheets, Bearaby for weighted blankets, Dr. Squatch for deodorant and grooming kits, Spot & Tango for dog food. Only Helix and Nike appeared in both the answer and the ad. Bearaby's ad showed a US shopper a price in British pounds.

We ran ChatGPT logged out, so these ads may differ for signed in users and paid plans.

## What this means for your brand this Black Friday

1. **Put the offer on your homepage and a sale page in plain text.** The discount, the code, the dates and who it applies to. Google AI Mode found these. Make sure the text is in the page HTML, not only in an image or popup.
2. **Get into the roundups ChatGPT reads.** ChatGPT mostly repeated deals that publishers had already listed. Send your offer to the deal editors at the sites that cover your category before Black Friday week, and keep it current when it changes.
3. **Spell out the terms.** First order only, subscription only, minimum spend, end date. When AI knew the terms, it passed them on, and shoppers trust an answer that tells them the catch.
4. **Watch which codes AI is quoting for you.** AI shared influencer and publisher codes as if they were the brand's offer. If an affiliate code gives a different discount from your main sale, AI may send shoppers to it.
5. **Don't put evergreen discounts in product names.** A bundle named "20% Off" was presented as a Black Friday deal. If a discount runs all year, say so on the page.
6. **Keep Google's shopping data current.** Google AI Mode's product cards showed sale prices and sellers from Google's shopping results. If your feed shows the sale price and your store as the seller, the card can be yours.
7. **Ask the AIs yourself this week.** Ask the way a shopper would ("Which natural deodorant brands are doing Black Friday deals?") in both ChatGPT and Google AI Mode, and check whether you're named and whether the deal is right.

## How we ran this test

- **Questions:** 20 Black Friday questions written the way US shoppers ask, across fitness, food and drink, supplements, beauty and personal care, home, pets, apparel, travel and gifts (for example: "I have a big dog. What Black Friday deals are there on dog food or dog treats?"). Full list in [black_friday_ai_answers.csv](/data/reports/black-friday-ai-deals-2026/black_friday_ai_answers.csv).
- **AIs:** Google AI Mode (US and English settings, signed in to a Google account) and ChatGPT (logged out, default model). Each question asked once per AI. 40 answers in total.
- **Date:** November 24, 2026, three days before Black Friday.
- **What we recorded:** brands named, product cards (product, price and seller), deal claims and discount codes, the sources shown, and any sponsored placement.
- **Deal checks:** 9 deals checked against the brand's own homepage on the same day. We counted a deal as right when the discount on the homepage matched what the AI said.
- **Gemini:** We also ran these questions in Gemini, but its answers were personalized to the signed in account's location settings rather than a US shopper, so Gemini isn't included in this report.
- **Limits:** One snapshot, three days before Black Friday. Deals change daily this week. Google AI Mode shows only a few sources before "Show all," so source notes cover what was visible on screen. Brand counts include every brand named in the answer, and ChatGPT counts exclude brands that appeared only in the ad.
