**By Carson Cobb. Data collected November 4 and 5, 2026.**

When ChatGPT recommends products, it rarely just lists them. It labels them: "Best overall", "Best value", "Best for dry skin", "Best for big dogs that pull". That label is often the first thing a shopper reads, and it decides which pick fits them.

I went back to the answers from my [Who ChatGPT recommends](/reports/who-chatgpt-recommends-2026) research, 48 buyer questions asked in ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode, and counted every label each AI gave.

## The short version

- **ChatGPT labels almost everything.** 44 of 48 answers had at least one "best for" style label, 149 labels in total.
- **Gemini and AI Mode label less.** 31 and 29 answers.
- **Most labels name a specific need.** 45% of ChatGPT's labels and 51% of AI Mode's described a shopper situation, not "best overall".
- **"Best overall" is one spot per answer.** It went to about a third of ChatGPT's labels.
- **Value and budget labels are the next biggest group.** 17% of ChatGPT's labels.

## How often each AI labels picks

| | ChatGPT | Gemini | Google AI Mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Answers with at least one label | 44 of 48 | 31 of 48 | 29 of 48 |
| Total labels | 149 | 66 | 76 |

## What kind of labels

| Label type | ChatGPT | Gemini | Google AI Mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Specific need ("best for dry skin") | 67 (45%) | 26 (39%) | 39 (51%) |
| Best overall or top pick | 51 (34%) | 28 (42%) | 22 (29%) |
| Value or budget | 25 (17%) | 11 (17%) | 11 (14%) |
| Premium or splurge | 6 (4%) | 1 (2%) | 4 (5%) |

Examples of specific need labels from the answers: best for natural fiber comfort, best for muscular legs, best for extreme cold, best for oily skin, best for hot sleepers, best for hard water, best for septic systems, best for trail running, best for gifting.

## What this means

There's one "best overall" in each answer, and it usually goes to the brand everyone already knows. But there are two or three specific need labels in the same answer, and those are much more winnable. A smaller brand that's clearly the best option for one kind of shopper has a real path into the answer.

Labels also show what AI understood about a product. If ChatGPT calls your moisturizer "best for oily skin" when you built it for dry skin, the information it found about you is unclear or contradictory. See [what AI gets wrong about your products](/reports/what-ai-gets-wrong-about-products-2026).

Where do the labels come from? Mostly from the sources AI cites: review sites and testing roundups that already sort products into "best for" groups, plus the product's own pages. See [the sources ChatGPT cites](/reports/sources-chatgpt-cites-2026).

## What to do with this

1. **Pick the need you want to own.** One clear "best for" per product, chosen from what your customers actually say.
2. **Say it plainly on the product page.** "Made for dogs over 50 lbs who pull" in the first lines, not buried in the description.
3. **Back it with specifics.** Ingredients, materials, sizes, test results: the details that make the claim checkable.
4. **Ask reviewers to describe themselves.** "I have oily skin and this didn't break me out" helps AI match you to the next oily skin shopper.
5. **Get the same label from outside.** Pitch the "best for" angle to the roundups and review sites in your space.
6. **Check what AI calls you now.** Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and AI Mode for the best products in your space and note the label next to your name, if you're there.

## How this was done

- **Answers:** the 48 short buyer questions from [Who ChatGPT recommends](/reports/who-chatgpt-recommends-2026), 8 in each of six product areas, asked in ChatGPT on November 4, 2026 and in Gemini and Google AI Mode on November 5, 2026, each in a fresh session.
- **Labels:** phrases such as "best overall", "best value", "best for ...", "budget pick" and "top pick" in the answer text, grouped by type with keyword rules and checked by hand. Questions asking "which is best for you" were excluded.
- **Limits:** 48 questions per AI. Labels depend on the wording of the question, so a question that names a need ("for sensitive skin") gets more need based labels. See [how question wording changes AI recommendations](/reports/how-question-wording-changes-ai-recommendations).

No store or brand is named in this report.
