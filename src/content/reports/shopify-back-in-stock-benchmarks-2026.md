**By Carson Cobb. Data collected November 16, 2026.**

A sold out product page is a shopper who already wanted to buy. Either you capture them, with a "notify me" form that brings them back when stock returns, or they buy the same thing somewhere else.

I checked the full catalog of 107 Shopify brand stores for products with every variant sold out, then opened one sold out product page per store and looked for a back in stock signup.

## The short version

- **Most stores have something sold out.** 77 of 105 catalogs (73%) had at least one fully sold out product.
- **It's a small share of the catalog.** The median store had 4% of products sold out.
- **36% of sold out pages capture nothing.** 28 of the 77 pages I checked had no way to get notified.
- **64% offered a signup.** 49 of 77.
- **Home and pet stores were the least likely to capture the shopper.** Beauty was the most likely.

The samples per area are small, so treat these as a direction, not a ranking. Even the best area left about a quarter of sold out pages with nothing.

## How stores capture the shopper

On the 49 pages with a signup, the most common setups were Klaviyo's back in stock feature and dedicated back in stock apps, with a few wishlist apps that double as alerts. I didn't compare apps, but the setups that work best share three things: the form appears on the exact sold out variant, it works on mobile, and the email goes out the day stock returns, not days later.

## Why a sold out page is worth fixing

A sold out product is often a best seller, which is why it sold out. Its page keeps getting traffic from search, ads, emails and AI answers that recommended it. Without a signup, every one of those visits ends there.

A back in stock list also tells you which products to reorder first and in what depth. And the email itself, sent to people who asked for it, is usually one of the best converting emails a store sends.

## What to do with this

1. **Check every sold out page, not just one.** Variants matter: a sold out size on an otherwise available product needs the form too.
2. **Turn it on.** If you use Klaviyo, its back in stock feature works with most themes. Otherwise use a dedicated app.
3. **Make the button obvious.** "Notify me when it's back" in place of the greyed out Add to Cart, not a link at the bottom.
4. **Offer an alternative.** Show a similar in stock product right below, for the shopper who can't wait.
5. **Keep sold out pages live if the product is coming back.** Hide or redirect only the ones that are gone for good.
6. **Watch the list size.** It's the cleanest demand signal you have for planning stock.

## How this was done

- **Stores:** 107 Shopify brand stores, 17 to 18 in each of six product areas (supplements, beauty, food and drink, pet, home, apparel), drawn at random from my dataset of 620 Shopify brand stores. Client stores were excluded.
- **Sold out products:** from each store's public product list (`/products.json`, up to 250 products, gift cards excluded), November 16, 2026. A product counted as sold out when every variant was unavailable. 105 stores returned a product list.
- **Back in stock signup:** the first sold out product page per store, checked for a visible signup ("notify me", "email me when available", "join the waitlist" and similar) or the script of a known back in stock tool.
- **Limits:** one sold out page per store. A store may show the form only after a variant is selected, or only on some products. Results describe the page as it loads, not every possible setup.

No store or brand is named in this report.
